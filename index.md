# Hi, I'm Alys

Welcome to my site. Myresearch focuses on using innovative physiological and computational models to improve analysis and interpretation of clinical imaging (ultrasound, computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging). I have a particular interest in health during pregnancy and improving interpretation of how changes in the complex anatomical micro-structure of developing or remodelling organs relates to whole organ function. Primary focuses of my research include oxygen exchange function, circulatory biomechanics, and remodelling and development of muscular organs. This includes uterine changes in reproduction and pregnancy, the structure and function of the placenta, and evolution the cardiovascular and pulmonary systems over the life-course.

## The placenta and pregnancy

The time before we are born sets the scene for our health later in life. Babies that are born too early or too small can have ongoing health issues, including neurodevelopmental delay resulting from impaired oxygen availability during critical periods of brain development. They are also at greater risk of later-life problems such as heart disease and diabetes. As the source of these problems relates to development prior to birth, it is critical to identify risk as early in pregnancy as possible. I co-lead a research group with A/Prof Jo James (Faculty of Medical and Health Sciences) focusing on better understanding the anatomy and function of the placenta, and cardiovascular adaptions to pregnancy. In our group we are developing a virtual pregnancy, or a pregnancy that lives on a computer, with the aim of improving knowledge of the physiology of pregnancy, and alongside this, risk detection.

## The uterus, pain and fertility

Smooth muscle organs exhibit rhythmic electrical activity that helps to coordinate organ function. In the uterus, this contributes to labour and delivery of the baby at the end of pregnancy, but is also important outside of pregnancy. Those with dysfunctional uterine electrical activity can have significant pain, are at more risk of conditions such as endometriosis, and fertility issues. With Prof Leo Cheng (Auckland Bioengineering Institute), I lead research into new ways to image and measure the uterus and its electrical activity. This research has at its foundation biophysical models of uterine smooth muscle (the contractile machinery of the uterus), and I am involved in research into other smooth muscle function within the human body that complements this focus on the uterus.

## Respiratory function through life

Our lungs take over from the placenta in supplying us with oxygen when we are born. I have a particular interest in how our respiratory system develops and later remodels in response to disease. I have conducted research, and supervised students in a diverse range of projects related to respiratory health, and has particular research interests in vascular pathology in the lungs, and ventilation strategies to assist patients with lung disease.
